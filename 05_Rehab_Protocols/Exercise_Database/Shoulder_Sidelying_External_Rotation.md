# Shoulder Sidelying External Rotation

## Identification status

Added based on the video "Side Lying Dumbbell External Rotation" by The Active Life.

The exercise isolates the posterior rotator cuff (infraspinatus and teres minor) — the external rotators of the shoulder — with the arm adducted to the side, which keeps capsular and labral load low.

## Basic info

- Name: Sidelying External Rotation
- Category: Strength / Rotator cuff isolation
- Exercise goal: rebuild strength and eccentric control of the posterior rotator cuff (infraspinatus, teres minor) under low joint load.
- Source: The Active Life
- Video:
  - https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=brwBvldn4gw

## Goal

Targeted activation and strengthening of the posterior rotator cuff (infraspinatus + teres minor). A core post-op rebuild exercise: the side-lying position with the arm adducted minimizes capsular load while keeping the external rotators highly active. Particularly relevant for restoring eccentric control in the posterior structures, which the current cycle has flagged as a weak link (reactive pain on eccentric phase of prone I-Y-T in the T position).

## Cues

- Elbow stays pinned to the side; place a rolled towel between elbow and ribs for alignment
- Only the forearm moves; the upper arm stays still
- Don't help with the torso — body remains fully on its side, no rolling back
- Controlled tempo, no jerks or momentum from the dumbbell
- Stay within a comfortable external rotation range — don't force end-range

## Ideal performance

Reference: see `reference_url` in frontmatter.

- Lie on your side, working arm on top, body stacked.
- Bend the working elbow to 90°, forearm across the stomach.
- Place a rolled towel between the elbow and the ribs to keep the arm slightly off the torso.
- Hold a light dumbbell in the working hand.
- Slowly rotate the forearm outward (away from the stomach), keeping the elbow glued to the towel.
- Pause briefly at the top of the comfortable range.
- Lower under control over 3 seconds back to the starting position.

## Performance recommendations

- Frequency: 1× per day
- Sets: 2–3
- Reps: 10–15
- Tempo: 2s concentric / 3s eccentric (emphasis on the return phase)
- Weight: start with 0.5–1 kg; progress slowly (up to 2–3 kg max for late-phase rehab without PT clearance for higher load)
- Range of motion: comfortable ER only — do not force
- Pauses: 1s hold at the end of the rotation

## What to track

- pain before / after (per direction if applicable)
- approximate external rotation range
- smoothness of movement; presence of jerks
- whether elbow stays in contact with the towel/ribs
- torso compensation (rolling back to assist)
- scapular compensation (winging, anterior tilt)
- neck / upper trapezius compensation (shrugging)
- subjective difficulty
- fear of movement / sense of instability

## Common errors

- Letting the elbow drift away from the body
- Rolling the torso back to gain range
- Using a dumbbell that's too heavy and recruiting deltoid / momentum
- Shrugging the shoulder toward the ear
- Forcing range past the comfortable end-point
- Rushing the eccentric (the most valuable phase of the rep)
- Holding breath through the set

## Restrictions

What is forbidden or undesirable. Source (doctor / PT / self):

- Do not push through pain > 2/10.
- Stop and consult PT if the pain quality is `sharp`, `pinching`, `electric`, or located in the front of the joint.
- Do not progress weight rapidly — posterior cuff fatigues quickly and form degrades before the user notices.
- Self-imposed: given history of posterior subluxation + bone graft + SLAP repair, do not chase end-range external rotation in this position; rebuild strength first, range later.

## Conditions for adding to a cycle

- Minimum recovery stage: `early` (suitable from early phase onward)
- Required equipment: light dumbbell (0.5–3 kg) and a rolled towel — both available
- Whether protocol approval/prescription is required: recommended to confirm load progression with PT, especially for the first cycle of inclusion
- Other conditions: best paired with at least one closed-chain stabilization exercise to balance the open-chain isolation work
