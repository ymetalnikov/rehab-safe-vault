## Basic info

- Name: Thoracic extension stretch
- Category: Mobility / thoracic spine / shoulder girdle prep
- Exercise goal: improve thoracic extension so the shoulder can move overhead more easily without excess compensation through the lower back, neck, or shoulder
- Source: E3 Rehab — Shoulder Dislocation & Instability Rehab
- Video:
  - https://youtu.be/fbV6EpgXIaw

## Exercise idea

Limited thoracic mobility can interfere with shoulder movement and provoke compensations. This exercise isn't a strength exercise for the shoulder: the goal is to gently open the thoracic spine and improve trunk position for subsequent shoulder movements.

## Variants

### Variant 1 — Thoracic extension over foam roller

Thoracic extension over a foam roller.

- Lie on your back.
- Place the roller across the thoracic spine, not under the lower back.
- Support the head with the hands if needed.
- Gently open the thoracic spine back over the roller.
- Don't arch aggressively in the lower back.
- You can perform a few calm reps or hold a comfortable position.

What to track:

- sensation of stretch in the thoracic spine;
- absence of pain in the shoulder;
- whether the movement isn't shifting into the lower back;
- whether it becomes easier to lift the arm afterward.

### Variant 2 — Bench / chair thoracic extension stretch

Thoracic extension with support on a bench, chair, or elevated surface.

- Kneel in front of the bench / chair.
- Place elbows or hands on the support.
- Gently lower the chest down between the arms.
- Keep the movement in the thoracic spine, not just the lower back.
- Move within a comfortable range.

What to track:

- chest opening;
- shoulder comfort;
- whether there's excessive lower-back arching;
- symmetry of sensation between right and left.

### Variant 3 — Quadruped / floor thoracic extension stretch

A floor variant from quadruped or with hands supported in front.

- Get on all fours or assume a similar position with hands supporting.
- Gently take the chest down and back.
- Keep the neck relaxed.
- Don't push the shoulder through pain.
- Use this variant as a more accessible option if a roller or bench is uncomfortable.

What to track:

- where the movement is felt: thoracic / shoulder / lower back;
- whether there's pain or instability in the shoulder;
- whether you can relax the neck and traps;
- whether this variant can be used before shoulder range exercises.

## What to track overall

- subjective thoracic stiffness before/after;
- whether overhead shoulder movements become easier;
- whether there's lumbar compensation;
- whether there's tension in the neck/traps;
- whether shoulder pain appears;
- which of the three variants is most comfortable and reproducible.

## Common errors

- trying to "force" the range through pain;
- extending in the lower back instead of the thoracic spine;
- tensing the neck and shrugging the shoulders;
- using too aggressive a variant too early;
- treating the exercise as a shoulder strength test rather than a gentle mobilization.

## Importance

Cycle weight: `2` — supplementary exercise for shoulder movement quality.

Why it matters:

- can reduce compensations during overhead movements;
- helps prepare the trunk and shoulder girdle;
- offers a simple way to see whether the thoracic spine affects shoulder feel;
- you can pick the most comfortable of three variants.

## Restrictions and conditions

- Don't perform through pain in the shoulder, neck, or back.
- Don't use aggressive extension if it worsens symptoms.
- Don't treat the exercise as mandatory for all protocols.
- If the shoulder feels worse after a variant — note it in the journal and pick another variant or remove the exercise.
