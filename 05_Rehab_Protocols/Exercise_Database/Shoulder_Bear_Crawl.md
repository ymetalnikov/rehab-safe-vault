# Shoulder Bear Crawl (Scapular Focus)

## Goal

Develop high-level dynamic scapular stability and serratus anterior strength. This exercise mimics the "active shoulder" needed in climbing when moving between holds under load.

## Cues

- **Push the Floor Away:** Actively press your hands into the ground to spread your shoulder blades apart (protraction). Your upper back should be slightly "domed."
- **Knee Hover:** Keep your knees only 2-5 cm off the ground.
- **Micro-steps:** Move opposite hand and foot simultaneously in very small, controlled steps.
- **Flat Back:** Maintain a "tabletop" spine. No dipping or arching.
- **Quiet Hips:** Minimize swaying or rocking. All stability comes from the core and shoulders.

## Ideal performance

1. **Starting Position:** On all fours (quadruped). Hands under shoulders, knees under hips.
2. **Action:** Lift knees slightly off the floor. Push through the hands to engage the serratus.
3. **Movement:** Crawl forward 2-3 meters, then backward. Maintain the "domed" back throughout.

## Performance recommendations

- Frequency: 3-4 times per week
- Sets: 3
- Reps: 30-60 seconds (or 5 meters forward/back)
- Time: N/A
- Tempo: Very slow and deliberate
- Range of motion: Full extension of the supporting arm
- Pauses: 60 seconds

## What to track

- scapular winging (do the blades pop out?)
- core collapse (does the lower back arch?)
- shoulder pain (especially on the weight-bearing side)
- fatigue levels
- smoothness of limb transitions

## Common errors

- **Winging:** Letting the chest sink between the shoulder blades.
- **High Hips:** Lifting the butt too high, shifting weight off the shoulders.
- **Large Steps:** Taking big steps that cause the torso to rotate.
- **Neck Tension:** Looking up too far or letting the head hang.

## Restrictions

- Avoid if wrist pain occurs (can be done on fists/parallettes).
- Stop if sharp shoulder pain or "clunking" is felt during weight shifts.

## Conditions for adding to a cycle

- Minimum recovery stage: Late-phase.
- Required equipment: Flat floor.
- Whether protocol approval/prescription is required: Yes.
- Other conditions: 100% mastery of static scapular push-ups/planks.
