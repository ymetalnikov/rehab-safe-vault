# Shoulder Plyometrics (Wall Ball Tosses)

## Basic info

- Name: Shoulder Plyometrics (Wall Ball Tosses)
- Category: Plyometrics / Advanced Stability
- Exercise goal: Improve explosive power, reactive strength, and dynamic stability in the shoulder complex.
- Source: YouTube (Shoulder Plyometrics)
- Reference video/link: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=clfcqxHZQMg
- Description of ideal performance: Standing in a staggered stance with an engaged core, throwing a small weighted ball (1.5–2.5 kg) against the wall and catching it with a continuous, rhythmic motion.

## Importance

Exercise weight in the current cycle:

- `3` — useful (essential for Return to Sport or high-activity phases)

Why it matters:
- Trains the muscles to react quickly to external forces (reactive stability).
- Bridges the gap between standard strength training and real-world explosive movements.

## Performance recommendations

- Frequency: 2-3 times per week
- Sets: 2-3
- Reps: 30–60 seconds per set
- Time: N/A
- Tempo: Fast / Explosive
- Range of motion: Controlled throwing motion
- Pauses: 60-90 seconds between sets

## What to track

- pain before/after;
- rhythm consistency (was there a pause?);
- core stability (was the torso moving too much?);
- fatigue level;
- subjective difficulty.

## Common errors

- **Losing Core Engagement:** Torso "wobbling" during the throw or catch.
- **Breaking Rhythm:** Pausing between catching and throwing (should be one fluid motion).
- **Excessive Weight:** Using a ball that is too heavy, reducing the speed and plyometric benefit.
- **Working Through Sharp Pain:** Ignoring joint signals during high-speed movement.

## Restrictions

What is forbidden or undesirable:
- Do not perform if basic strength and pain-free range of motion are not yet established.
- Avoid if there is any joint clicking or sharp pain during the impact.

## Conditions for adding to a cycle

- Minimum recovery stage: Late stage (Return to Activity).
- Whether protocol approval/prescription is required: Mandatory.
- Which conditions must be met: Full pain-free range of motion and good baseline strength of the rotator cuff.
