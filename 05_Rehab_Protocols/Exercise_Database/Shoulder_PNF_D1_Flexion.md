# Shoulder PNF D1 Flexion

## Goal

Improve functional coordination, neuromuscular control, and multi-planar mobility. The D1 Flexion pattern (often called the "feeding" or "seatbelt" pattern) integrates shoulder flexion, adduction, and external rotation.

## Cues

- **Lead with the Thumb:** As you move up and across, rotate your hand so the thumb points toward your opposite ear.
- **Cross the Midline:** The movement must go diagonally across your body, not just straight up.
- **Wrist Integration:** Start with an open palm/extended wrist at the bottom, and finish with a closed fist/flexed wrist near the ear.
- **Smooth Diagonal:** Maintain a consistent diagonal path; do not "arc" or "circle" the movement.

## Ideal performance

This exercise can be performed with or without resistance (using a light resistance band).

1. **Starting Position (D1 Extension):** Stand tall. Position your arm down and slightly behind you, away from your body (as if reaching back for a seatbelt). Palm faces back/out.
2. **Action:** Bring your arm up and across your body toward the opposite ear.
3. **Rotation:** Simultaneously rotate your forearm (supination) so your palm faces your face at the top.
4. **Return:** Reverse the movement slowly back to the starting position.

## Performance recommendations

- Frequency: 2-3 times per week
- Sets: 2-3
- Reps: 10-12 per arm
- Time: N/A
- Tempo: Controlled and fluid (3 sec up, 3 sec down)
- Range of motion: Full diagonal range from hip to opposite ear.
- Pauses: 60 seconds between sets

## What to track

- pain before/after
- range of motion (ability to reach the opposite ear)
- smoothness of the diagonal path
- torso rotation (compensation — avoid twisting the spine)
- shrugging (compensation)
- coordination (fluidity of rotation)
- subjective difficulty

## Common errors

- **Straight Lifting:** Moving the arm in a straight plane (flexion or abduction) instead of a diagonal.
- **Lack of Rotation:** Failing to rotate the palm toward the face at the top.
- **Torso Twisting:** Using the trunk to "swing" the arm across.
- **Shrugging:** Using the upper trapezius to initiate the lift.

## Restrictions

- Avoid if it causes "pinching" or sharp pain in the front of the shoulder.
- Not recommended for early-stage rehab unless performed passively.

## Conditions for adding to a cycle

- Minimum recovery stage: Late-stage (full active range of motion mostly restored).
- Required equipment: Light resistance band (Level 1-2) or none.
- Whether protocol approval/prescription is required: Yes.
- Other conditions: Good baseline scapular control.
