# Shoulder Pectoralis Stretch

## Basic info

- Name: Shoulder Pectoralis Stretch
- Category: Stretching / Posture
- Exercise goal: Improve posture and shoulder mechanics by stretching the chest muscles (Pectoralis Major/Minor).
- Source: YouTube (Shoulder Pectoralis Stretch)
- Reference video/link: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IZpJu7A_itk
- Description of ideal performance: Standing next to a doorway with the arm at a 90-degree angle and palm on the frame. Slowly rotate the torso away from the wall until a gentle stretch is felt in the chest.

## Importance

Exercise weight in the current cycle:

- `3` — useful (helps maintain shoulder mobility and correct posture)

Why it matters:
- Tight chest muscles pull the shoulders forward, increasing joint impingement.
- Essential for restoring full range of motion in external rotation and abduction.

## Performance recommendations

- Frequency: 1-2 times daily
- Sets: 2-3 per side
- Reps: 1 hold
- Time: 30 seconds
- Tempo: Static stretch
- Range of motion: Gentle pull, no sharp pain
- Pauses: 15-30 seconds

## What to track

- pain before/after;
- sensation of stretch (gentle vs sharp);
- arm angle (90 deg vs lower);
- torso rotation degree;
- subjective difficulty.

## Common errors

- **Sharp Pain:** Pushing into the stretch despite joint pain.
- **Aggressive Rotation:** Turning the body too fast or too far.
- **Incorrect Arm Height:** Not adjusting the arm angle for comfort (should lower it if painful).

## Restrictions

What is forbidden or undesirable:

- Do not perform if it causes sharp joint pain.
- Avoid "bouncing" (ballistic stretching).

## Conditions for adding to a cycle

- Minimum recovery stage: Mid-stage (or early if arm height is reduced).
- Whether protocol approval/prescription is required: Recommended.
- Which conditions must be met: Ability to abduct arm to ~70-90 degrees comfortably.
