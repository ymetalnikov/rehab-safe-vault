## Exercise idea

The user lies on their back and performs controlled shoulder external rotation without a dowel. The movement is performed only within a comfortable and permitted range, without jerks or a sense of instability.

## Reference performance

- Lie on your back.
- Position the working shoulder and elbow stably.
- Keep the elbow of the working arm bent at roughly a right angle.
- Slowly perform external rotation of the shoulder.
- Move only within a comfortable range.
- Don't help the movement with a torso or shoulder jerk.
- Return back slowly and under control.

## What to track

- pain before/after;
- approximate external rotation range;
- smoothness of movement;
- whether there are jerks;
- whether the elbow stays in a stable position;
- whether there's compensation through the torso, scapula, or neck;
- whether a sense of instability or fear appears;
- whether the range is increasing on trend.

## Common errors

- pushing through pain;
- rotating the shoulder sharply;
- letting the elbow drift from its set position;
- compensating with torso movement;
- shrugging the shoulder toward the ear;
- chasing range instead of control;
- ignoring a sense of instability.

## Importance

Cycle weight: `3` — important exercise for tracking external rotation range.

Why it matters:

- external rotation is often limited after shoulder injury or instability;
- the exercise is convenient to repeat at home;
- you can track range and movement confidence week over week;
- it complements supine shoulder flexion with a dowel well.

## Restrictions and conditions

- Don't treat as universally allowed for all surgeries, injuries, and recovery timeframes.
- Add to a cycle only if it's in the personal protocol or the user has decided to track it themselves.
- Don't force external rotation.
- Stop or reduce the range if pain, fear, or a sense of instability appears.
- If the video/angle is poor — don't draw confident conclusions about technique.
