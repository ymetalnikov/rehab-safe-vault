# Shoulder Stabilization on Ball at Wall

## Identification status

Added based on the video The Stone Clinic — Soccer Ball Shoulder Exercises.

The exercise targets dynamic stability of the scapula and shoulder joint through work on an unstable surface.

## Basic info

- Name: Soccer Ball Wall Stability Exercises (Wall Push-ups / Circles)
- Category: Stability / Proprioception / Scapular Control
- Exercise goal: improve scapular control (protraction) and dynamic shoulder stability on an unstable surface.
- Source: The Stone Clinic
- Video:
  - https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=a0PVwZ_a7Bc

## Exercise idea

Using a ball (soccer or similar) creates an unstable surface that forces the rotator cuff and serratus anterior to work harder. This is a progression from regular wall push-ups.

## Variants

### Variant 1 — Push-ups on the ball (two hands)

- Place the ball against the wall at chest level.
- Place both hands on the ball (you can form a "diamond" with the fingers for stability).
- Perform gentle push-ups, keeping the scapulae in **protraction** (push the torso forward, don't let the scapulae "drop" toward the spine).

### Variant 2 — Circles (two hands)

- Holding the ball against the wall with both hands, perform small circles clockwise and counterclockwise.
- Maintain control over the center of mass.

### Variant 3 — Circles (one hand) — Progression

- Perform circles with one hand.
- **The elbow must be fully extended** so the work is driven by the shoulder and scapular stabilizers.

## What to track

- **Scapular protraction:** are the scapulae winging, is the chest collapsing inward.
- **Smoothness of circles:** the movement should be controlled, not chaotic.
- **Elbow stability:** in the one-hand variant, the elbow must not bend.
- **Pain and discomfort:** absence of sharp pain or sense of joint slipping out.
- **Fatigue:** where does it set in faster (shoulder or scapular area).

## Common errors

- Scapular drop (loss of protraction).
- Bending the elbow during circles (especially in the one-hand progression).
- Excessive circle amplitude leading to loss of control.
- Shrugging the shoulders toward the ears (trapezius tension).

## Importance

Cycle weight: `4` — important exercise for the late stages of restoring control and preparing for load.

Why it matters:
- develops proprioception (sense of joint position in space);
- strengthens the serratus anterior (important for proper scapular rhythm);
- creates conditions for safe loading in a closed kinetic chain.

## Restrictions and conditions

- Do not perform during acute pain.
- Do not progress to the one-hand variant until two-handed control is solid.
- Make sure the ball has good grip against both the wall and the hands.
